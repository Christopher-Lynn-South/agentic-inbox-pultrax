Method note: Registration status was checked by RDAP on 2026-10-10. .com/.net used Verisign (`rdap.verisign.com`), .co used `rdap.registry.co`, and .us used `rdap.nic.us`. "Appeared unregistered via RDAP on 2026-10-10" means an HTTP 404. It is NOT a guarantee of availability: domains can be in redemption or pending delete, premium-reserved, or registered minutes later. Live/parked status came from `curl` of http/https plus the page title. Port-43 WHOIS was blocked in the research environment, so registrant identities were not retrieved (most are privacy-redacted anyway). ## 2. Trademark risk of "Overhead Door" in a domain or business name

### Takeaway
Using "Overhead Door" as a name element carries **real, documented enforcement risk**. Overhead Door Corporation (ODC, a Sanwa Holdings subsidiary) and its authorized distributors (who operate as "Overhead Door Company of [City]") have repeatedly sent cease-and-desist letters and sued over trade names and domains containing "Overhead Door". ODC claims rights in "OVERHEAD DOOR", "OVERHEAD DOOR COMPANY" and even "OVERHEAD" standing alone. Descriptive use of "overhead doors" in body copy is defensible. Putting it in the **brand name or domain** (azoverheaddoors.com, arizonaoverheaddoors.com, "AZ Overhead Door") is the risky pattern. Safer phrasing: "commercial doors", "sectional doors", "roll-up doors", "dock doors", "garage & commercial doors".

### Cited Findings
- ODC holds a federal registration for OVERHEAD DOOR (cited as covering garage door openers) plus a wordless "banner" (red ribbon) mark. ODC claims rights in "OVERHEAD DOOR," "OVERHEAD DOOR COMPANY," and "OVERHEAD" "as used without any other words." Per the court record summarized by Tushnet, ODC "has regularly taken legal action to prevent use by others of OVERHEAD DOOR or OVERHEAD, as company names" — [Tushnet, OGD Equipment Co. v. Overhead Door Corp., E.D. Tex. No. 17-cv-00898 (2019)](https://tushnet.com/2019/10/25/company-claiming-rights-in-overhead-doors-makes-little-headway-against-challenger/)
- In July 2017 ODC sent Overhead Garage Door (OGD, Texas) a cease-and-desist calling its uses "clear trademark infringement violations … under Texas and Federal law." OGD sought a declaration that "overhead"/"overhead door(s)" are generic, and alleged ODC told consumers only ODC and its distributors may lawfully use the names. Some claims survived a motion to dismiss — [Tushnet](https://tushnet.com/2019/10/25/company-claiming-rights-in-overhead-doors-makes-little-headway-against-challenger/). Search-result summaries report the parties settled in Nov 2019 and that OGD's "Overhead Garage Door" logo registration was cancelled in 2021 — [Finnegan PDF, D.H. Pace Co. v. OGD Equipment](https://www.finnegan.com/a/web/vavmCEEx1CJHsTsnwjBkY5/dh-pace-co-v-ogd-equipment.pdf) (I did not read the full PDF.)
- D.H. Pace Co., doing business as "Overhead Door Company of Atlanta" (an ODC distributor since 1981, which says it spent over $8.5M advertising the brand in a decade), sued Aaron Overhead Door Atlanta LLC. Pace alleged Aaron's **domain name**, social handles and trade name misled consumers into assuming an affiliation. Pace sent C&D letters in March/April 2017, and demand letters to **ten other Georgia companies**. The case was N.D. Ga. No. 1:17-cv-3430, 522 F. Supp. 3d 1315 (Feb. 12, 2020 order). ODC's authorized distributors "may use its 'Overhead Door' trade names" — [vLex, D.H. Pace Co. v. Aaron Overhead Door Atlanta LLC](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/d-h-pace-co-899561264)
- The Eleventh Circuit revived Pace's (licensee) claims, holding that a licensee could sue even without owning the marks — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/garage-door-trademark-licensees-claims-revived-by-11th-cir). A search-result summary (not full text) says an earlier settlement barred Aaron from using "Overhead" in an Atlanta trade name and from using "Overhead Door(s)" in advertising "except in a purely descriptive sense."
- Overhead Door Corp. v. Burger (M.D. Ga. 2013–14): a default judgment for trademark infringement against a former distributor who kept using OVERHEAD DOOR after termination — [Willamette IP summary](https://wlo.willamette.edu/ip/2013/06/overhead-door-corp.-v.-burger.html); [Studicata](https://www.studicata.com/summaries/united-states-district-court-middle-district-of-georgia/overhead-door-corporation-v-burger-2014-h8zcjh/)
- Counterpoint: a 2007 email from ODC's own counsel, quoted in a court filing, conceded ODC lacks exclusive rights to "Overhead"/"Overhead Door" in every context and that stopping descriptive ad use would be hard without evidence of confusion or logo use (search-result summary of the [D.H. Pace/OGD record](https://www.finnegan.com/a/web/vavmCEEx1CJHsTsnwjBkY5/dh-pace-co-v-ogd-equipment.pdf); not independently verified in the full text).
- ODC also owns a 2021 design registration (No. 6264323) for a blue/red logo covering retail and installation services — [TrademarkElite](https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/88836070/logo)
- Arizona has an existing ODC distributor, "Overhead Door Company of Central Arizona" (Tempe), per a search-result summary of a job listing — [jofdav listing](https://www.jofdav.com/jobs/28824267-residential-installer). That makes AZ a market where a licensee has standing and a motive to enforce.

### Inferences
- azoverheaddoors.com appeared unregistered, but registering it as the **primary brand** invites a C&D from ODC or its Tempe distributor. "AZ Overhead Doors" is the same pattern as "Aaron Overhead Door Atlanta." The plural/generic argument ("overhead doors" = a product category) is legally plausible, but it has been litigated and settled rather than decided, so it is costly to rely on. Recommendation: do not use "overhead" in the brand name or primary domain. If wanted at all, use "overhead doors" only descriptively in page copy ("we install commercial overhead doors and roll-up doors"), never styled as a name, never with red-ribbon colors.
- Safer category words for the commercial line: "Commercial Doors", "Roll-Up Doors", "Sectional & Dock Doors", "Garage & Commercial Doors".
- Not legal advice. Have a trademark attorney run a USPTO knockout search on the chosen name.

### Gaps
- The USPTO TSDR record (registration number, current status, goods/services) for the OVERHEAD DOOR word mark was not retrieved directly. Verify at tsdr.uspto.gov.
- The exact settlement text in the Aaron/Pace case was not obtained from a primary source.

## 3. Exact-match / keyword domains vs. brandable domains for SEO and LLM recall in 2026

### Takeaway
Keyword domains give little or no direct ranking boost in Google. The 2012 EMD update removed the benefit for low-quality exact-match sites, and Google's John Mueller has repeatedly said keyword domains have no "recognizable SEO advantage." Keywords in the domain still help click-through, relevance cues and descriptive anchor text, so a **brand + category hybrid** ("[Distinctive] Garage Doors" / "[Distinctive] Door Co") is the best balance. For LLM/AI recommendations, what matters is a distinctive, consistent entity name repeated across third-party sources (GBP, directories, reviews, press). A generic descriptive name like "AZ Garage Doors" is hard for both Google and LLMs to separate from the category and from competitors (e.g. A1's arizonagaragedoors.com).

### Cited Findings
- Google's EMD update was announced by Matt Cutts on Sept 28, 2012. It targeted low-quality exact-match-domain sites, affected 0.6% of English-US queries noticeably, and was separate from Panda/Penguin. Moz observed a net 36 of 1,000 tracked domains drop out of the top 10 — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-emd-update-15776.html); [Search Engine Journal algorithm history](https://searchenginejournal.com/google-algorithm-history/emd-update)
- Mueller (Reddit, Dec 2023): "A keyword domain name is not going to give you any recognizable SEO advantage on Google," and he advised a brandable name for long-term use — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/john-mueller-on-keyword-domain-names-and-branding-for-seo/504786). Earlier (2018): "just because keywords are in a domain name doesn't mean that it'll automatically rank for those keywords" — [SEJ, keywords in domain name](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/keywords-in-domain-name/); [Local Search Forum thread](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/google-there-is-no-seo-advantage-to-keyword-domains.53017/)
- A secondary SEO-blog view: keyword domains can attract keyword-rich anchor text when people link using the bare name — [SEJ coverage](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/keywords-in-domain-name/) (opinion, not Google)
- LLM/AI visibility (vendor sources, directional only): consistent naming and category association across web, directories and partner listings correlates with more LLM mentions — [Paul Okhrem GEO benchmarks 2026](https://paul-okhrem.com/geo-benchmarks-2026/). A claimed 85% of LLM brand mentions come from third-party pages (secondhand AirOps figure) — [inblog glossary](https://inblog.ai/glossary/llm-visibility). Gemini visibility is tied to Google Business Profile signals and other assistants source differently — [Truelogic](https://www.truelogic.com.ph/blog/brand-visibility-ai-search/). Caveat: all are agency/vendor content, with no peer-reviewed local-business study found.

### Inferences
- In local service SEO, the Google Business Profile name, address, reviews and proximity drive Map Pack rankings more than the domain does. A keyword-stuffed business name on GBP violates GBP guidelines if it is not the real legal/DBA name, which is another reason to make the legal name a brand + category hybrid.
- For LLM recall, a distinctive token ("Saguaro", "Ocotillo") lets models attach reviews, citations and service facts to one entity. "AZ Garage Door Co" will blur with "AZ Garage Doors N More", "Arizona Garage Doors" (A1), etc.
- A descriptive name is also weak or unregistrable as a trademark (merely descriptive plus geographically descriptive), so the brand could not stop copycats. A suggestive or arbitrary element ("Saguaro") makes it protectable.

### Gaps
- No primary, quantitative 2025–2026 study isolating domain-name effects on local rankings or LLM recommendations was found.

## 5. Recommendation: top 3 domains + secondary domains

### Takeaway
Top 3 primary brands, all appearing unregistered via RDAP on 2026-10-10, with no AZ garage-door name conflicts found in web search:
1. **Saguaro Garage Doors — saguarogaragedoors.com** (defensive: saguarodoorco.com, saguarogaragedoor.com, saguarodoors.com, saguarogaragedoors.net/.co/.us)
2. **AZ Door Works — azdoorworks.com** (defensive: .net/.co/.us)
3. **Ocotillo Door Co. — ocotillodoorco.com** (defensive: ocotillogaragedoors.com)

Plus descriptive secondary domains: **azcommercialdoors.com / arizonacommercialdoors.com** (commercial line), **puertasdegarajeaz.com** (Spanish), **yumagaragedoors.com** (HQ market).

### Rationale
1. **Saguaro Garage Doors** (saguarogaragedoors.com)
   - SEO: carries the head term "garage doors" in the name and domain (CTR and anchor-text benefit), while "Saguaro" is the distinctive brand token. Every TLD variant appeared open, so the full set can be locked up.
   - LLM recall: the saguaro is an instantly Arizona-coded symbol (state flower = saguaro blossom), memorable and unambiguous as an entity, and it does not tie the brand to Yuma, so it scales statewide.
   - Trademark: "Saguaro" is arbitrary/suggestive for doors, so it is protectable. No AZ garage-door business using it was found. Weakness: the name says nothing about commercial doors, so pair it with the tagline "Garage & Commercial Doors" and a /commercial section or the azcommercialdoors.com redirect. Saguaro is common in other AZ business names, so check eCorp.
2. **AZ Door Works** (azdoorworks.com)
   - Short (11 characters), easy to say on radio/trucks, and "Door" rather than "Garage Door" covers residential, commercial, roll-up and dock doors in one brand. "AZ" signals statewide scope.
   - Risk: arizonadoorworks.com has been registered since 2009 and points to an "ArizonaDoorWorks" page, so there is a possible prior user. The name is also more descriptive and therefore weaker as a trademark and as an LLM entity. Verify in eCorp/ROC before choosing.
3. **Ocotillo Door Co.** (ocotillodoorco.com)
   - Distinctive, Sonoran-desert-native (ocotillo grows around Yuma), and very low conflict risk (no businesses found). "Door Co." covers commercial work.
   - Weakness: harder to spell and pronounce (oh-koh-TEE-yoh), and no "garage" keyword. Mitigate with ocotillogaragedoors.com as a redirect. Bilingual-friendly because the word is Spanish-origin.
- Not recommended as the primary brand: azgaragedoorco.com (open, but a generic descriptive name that blurs with A1's arizonagaragedoors.com, the HugeDomains azgaragedoors.com and "AZ Garage Doors N More", with weak trademark protection), and azoverheaddoors.com (open, but ODC trademark risk, see section 2).

### Secondary domains and redirect/microsite best practice
- **Commercial line**: register azcommercialdoors.com and arizonacommercialdoors.com (both appeared unregistered). Either 301-redirect them to /commercial-doors/ on the main site, or (only if the commercial division has genuinely distinct content, staff and phone) run a full site. Avoid "overhead" in these names.
- **Spanish**: puertasdegarajeaz.com (all TLDs open), puertasdegarajeyuma.com and puertasdegarajearizona.com. Best practice: host Spanish on the main domain (/es/) with hreflang en-US/es-US, and 301 the Spanish domains to /es/ for print, radio and Spanish-language ads. That consolidates authority instead of splitting it.
- **City/market domains** (yumagaragedoors.com is open, also yumadoorco.com, yumacommercialdoors.com, coloradoriverdoors.com): use them as **301 redirects to real location pages** (e.g. /yuma/) or for offline tracking (vanity URLs on trucks and mailers). Do **not** build thin near-duplicate city microsites. Google's spam policy explicitly lists "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse — [Google Search Central spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). Redirected keyword domains pass users, not a ranking boost (per section 3).
- **Typo/defensive**: grab .net/.co/.us of the chosen primary (all open for Saguaro / AZ Door Works / Ocotillo) and 301 them to the .com.
- Optional opportunistic buy: yumagaragedoors.com is open now (register it). The aftermarket azgaragedoors.com (HugeDomains) or arizonaoverheaddoors.com (Afternic) are not worth buying as the primary brand given the confusion and trademark issues.
